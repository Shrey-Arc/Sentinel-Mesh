SYSTEMS ENGINEERING ASSESSMENT

Modular Autonomous UAV Fleet Ecosystem

A comprehensive technical evaluation of transitioning from single monolithic drones to distributed, multi-tier aerial infrastructure. Based on physics-grounded models, actuator disc theory, and empirical hardware validation.

01. The Fundamental Physics Constraint

The defining limitation of modern commercial UAVs is the actuator disc theory constraint. Hover power for rotary-wing platforms scales as Mass^1.5, not linearly. Attempting to add payload (sensors, heavy AI compute, larger batteries) to a single airframe triggers a compounding penalty curve, destroying mission endurance. This mathematically forces the transition to a modular fleet architecture.

Engineering Fact: Adding a 470g payload to a 1.5kg baseline drone increases mass by 31%, but required hover power surges by 50.5%, slashing endurance from 65 minutes to 43 minutes.

02. Multi-Tier Fleet Architecture

By acknowledging that endurance is a fleet property rather than a per-drone property, the architecture decouples sensing, computation, and energy logistics. Heavy workloads are offloaded to specialized nodes, keeping edge sensors exceptionally light and efficient.

🛸

Worker Node

  • Mass: 0.5 - 0.8 kg
  • Role: Sensing & Flight
  • Endurance: 45 - 70 min

Stripped of heavy processing. Executes classical control loops and captures data.

📡

Command Node

  • Mass: 1.3 - 1.5 kg
  • Role: AI & Routing
  • Endurance: 35 - 50 min

Houses Jetson Orin NX. Handles VLA inference, mesh network relay, and fleet tasking.

⚡

Carrier Node

  • Mass: 3.0 - 25.0 kg
  • Role: Logistics
  • Endurance: 60 - 120 min

Mobile docking and battery rotation. Sustains continuous 7+ hour system-level operations.

04. Autonomy: The Reality of Edge AI

Despite industry hype surrounding zero-shot Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, empirical benchmarking reveals unacceptable latency and failure rates for flight control. The architecture deliberately utilizes a 'Supervised Autonomy' model using the classical SPF (See, Point, Fly) framework for low-level navigation, leaving LLMs to high-level intent parsing.

SPF (Classical + Vision)

Yields 92.7% success at 67ms latency. Safe for flight control loops.

GPT-4o Agent

Yields only 31.5% success at 245ms latency. Dangerous for low-level flight control.

Energy-Aware Scheduling

Throttling AI inference resolution at lower SOC values experimentally extends total mission time by ~11%.

05. Technical Evolution & Trajectory

Tracking the project's evolution from optimizing a singular platform to developing a distributed, persistent aerial infrastructure.

Phase 1: Platform Optimization

Implementation of teardrop laminar fuselage, FOC ESCs, and vibration isolation. Attempted to solve baseline endurance via aerodynamic and electromechanical efficiency.

Implemented / Validated

Phase 2: Modular Decoupling

Hitting the mass limits of single frames led to splitting payload rails. Separation of the lightweight "Worker" drone for sensing from the heavy compute payload.

Engineering Proposal

Phase 3: Fleet Ecosystem

Introduction of Command nodes for edge AI (Jetson Orin) and Carrier nodes for battery logistics. Implementation of LoRa/ESP-NOW mesh and energy-aware task scheduling.

Experimental Concept

Phase 4: Persistent Infrastructure

Drone-in-box systems, structural batteries, inductive charging spines, and true autonomous rotation enabling indefinite mission persistence.

Deferred / High-Risk
